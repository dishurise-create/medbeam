**Description**
Calling pharmacy after pharmacy to find your medication? MedBeam helps you know where to look first.

Search any prescription and your ZIP code. MedBeam shows nearby pharmacies on a map, each with an estimated chance of having your medication in stock.

HOW IT WORKS
• Official data: every estimate starts with the FDA Drug Shortages database for your exact medication and strength.
• More public data: MedBeam also looks at how commonly the medication is prescribed, how many companies make it, recent recalls and wholesale price trends.
• Community reports: after you check a pharmacy, tap "had it" or "didn't." Your anonymous answer helps neighbors find their medication too.

FEATURES
• Map of nearby pharmacies, ranked by best chance first
• A clear percentage for each pharmacy, and a breakdown of why
• Supply risk score for any medication
• Shortage Watch for the medications you save
• Early warnings when availability drops in your area
• Dark mode, text size and color options

PRIVATE BY DESIGN
No account. No name, prescription, doctor, diagnosis or insurance needed. MedBeam uses the ZIP code you type, not your GPS location.

IMPORTANT
MedBeam shows estimates, not live pharmacy inventory. Always call the pharmacy to confirm. MedBeam is an information tool, not medical advice. Never change your medication or dose without talking to your doctor or pharmacist.

## App Review Information
- **Sign-in required:** No
- **Contact:** your name, phone and dishu.rise@gmail.com
- **Notes:**
MedBeam helps people find prescription medications during shortages. No account or login is needed.

To test: enter a ZIP code (for example 20105), tap Medication and search "Adderall 20" or "amoxicillin 500", then tap Find pharmacies. Tap any pharmacy to see its estimate and the reasons behind it.

The percentages are estimates built from public U.S. government data (FDA Drug Shortages, FDA NDC Directory, FDA Enforcement Reports, Medicaid NADAC and Medicaid State Drug Utilization data), refined by optional anonymous user reports ("they had it" / "they didn't"). The app does not claim to show live pharmacy inventory, and says so on every estimate. It gives no medical advice and collects no personal or health information.

This app was built by a high school student for the 2026 Congressional App Challenge.
